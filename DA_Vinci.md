<h2 data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">How Green-Screen Keying Works: Step-by-Step<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></h2>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">The entire process relies on color isolation. In digital video, every pixel is made up of combinations of Red, Green, and Blue (RGB). Keying software targets a specific range of pure green pixels and instructs the computer to delete them.<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>
<h2 data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Step 1: The Production (Shooting)<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></h2>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">You place your subject in front of a flat, evenly lit green backdrop.<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">
<div>&nbsp;</div>
<ul>
<li data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep"><em data-sfc-root="ep">Why Green?<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></em> Digital camera sensors are highly sensitive to green, capturing it with the highest detail. More importantly, bright neon green is the furthest color from human skin tones, ensuring the software doesn&rsquo;t accidentally delete part of your face or arms. (Blue screens are also used, typically if the subject is wearing green clothing).<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></li>
</ul>
<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>
<h2 data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Step 2: The Keying (The Software Process)<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></h2>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Inside your video editor (like Filmora or VN), you apply the <span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Chroma Key<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> or <span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Smart Cutout<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> effect. You use an eyedropper tool to click on the green background. The software instantly isolates that color value and sets its opacity to 0% (making it clear like glass).<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>
<h2 data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Step 3: Compositing (Stacking Layers)<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></h2>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">You place your green-screen footage on <span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Video Track 2<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> (the top layer) and drop your new background image or video onto <span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Video Track 1<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> (the bottom layer). The new background instantly peeks through the transparent gaps, making it look like the subject is actually standing in that new location.<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>

<h2 data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Key Settings You Need to Tweak for a Perfect Clean Cut<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></h2>
<div data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">When you apply a chroma key in editors like Filmora, the cut is rarely perfect on the first click. You will see sliders to fine-tune the look:<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></div>

<li data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep"><span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Offset:<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> Adjusts the boundary edges of the cutout to distinguish between the background and your subject.<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></li>

<li data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep"><span data-sfc-cp="" data-sfc-root="ep">Spill Removal / Spill Suppression:<!--TgQPHd|||[]--></span> Green light naturally reflects off the backdrop onto the subject's shoulders and hair (called "green spill").
